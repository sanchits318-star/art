✨ Why HASS Matters ✨

Humanities and Social Sciences allow children to explore their world, understand people, places, and cultures, and develop critical thinking and problem-solving skills. Through HASS, young learners can investigate history, geography, civics, and community life, which encourages curiosity, imagination, and creativity. Activities such as storytelling, roleplay, and exploring maps or artefacts help children express ideas, make connections, and interpret the world in unique ways. Integrating creativity in HASS supports children to develop inquiry skills, collaborative learning, and the ability to see multiple perspectives, laying a foundation for informed, imaginative, and socially aware decision-making across the curriculum.

🧠 Creativity Theories 🧠

Piaget’s cognitive development theory highlights that creativity emerges as children actively explore, experiment, and construct knowledge about their environment. Vygotsky’s Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) emphasises how social interaction and scaffolding enhance creative thinking, allowing children to achieve tasks slightly beyond their current ability. Gardner’s Multiple Intelligences theory supports diverse ways of expressing creativity, including linguistic, interpersonal, and spatial intelligences, all relevant to HASS learning. Torrance’s creativity framework encourages originality, flexibility, and elaboration through open-ended problem-solving and exploration. These theories collectively position HASS as a curriculum area where children can investigate, interpret, and invent new ways to understand and express their world.

💭 Reflection 💭

My videos about family exploration, nature walks, and community helper roleplay make me feel proud of several aspects that went well. In the “Family Photo Exploration and Nature Walk” activity I successfully encouraged infants and toddlers to notice people, objects, and patterns. I guided them to point, touch, and describe what they saw, which helped them develop observation, language, and early social understanding. Children responded with curiosity and excitement, exploring leaves, stones, and flowers, and sharing their discoveries.

In the “Community Helper Roleplay and Story Mapping” activity, I provided costumes and props that inspired children to pretend and act out roles. They used imagination to explore jobs, take turns, and practice social interactions. Story mapping helped them understand sequences of events and encouraged creative thinking.

There were areas to improve. During the nature walk, some children needed more guidance to stay focused on the activity. I could have planned shorter segments or provided prompts to maintain attention. In the roleplay, a few children hesitated to engage fully. I could have modelled actions more explicitly and scaffolded participation. I will provide more visual demonstrations and structured prompts while allowing time for free exploration. I will also reflect with children by asking questions about what they observed and created. These strategies will enhance creativity, engagement, and social learning in young children.
